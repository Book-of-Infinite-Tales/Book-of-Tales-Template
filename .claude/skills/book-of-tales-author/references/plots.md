# Plot patterns

The shapes the published book's encounters take, drawn from its 132 character encounters and 162 milieu encounters. Use them as starting points, and fill them with your own people and places. Do not retell the published book's specific stories.

Each pattern lists its **setup**, the **fork** (the typical choices), the **skills** its resolutions tend to use, and the **renown tilt**.

## Character encounter patterns

### 1. The village trial
- **Setup:** villagers are trying one of their own, and the knight is asked to judge. For example: a miller who poached the lord's deer in a famine winter, a nurse accused of poisoning a lord she was paid to watch, a boy who rang the church bell to warn of raiders who never came.
- **Fork:** often three verdicts. **Mercy**, **the letter of the law**, and **blame the accuser** (the lord who set famine prices, the neighbour with a grudge).
- **Skills:** Diplomacy, Wisdom, Honor, Piety.
- **Renown:** mercy → Divinity; a harsh sentence can go either way; exposing the accuser → Divinity or Renown.
- **Note:** one of the book's most distinctive patterns. The best trials have no clean answer.

### 2. Help or rob
- **Setup:** someone vulnerable is carrying something valuable. For example: a pilgrim with a jewelled reliquary, a merchant's widow with the dowry chest, a lame horse-dealer with a purse of silver.
- **Fork:** help them / rob them.
- **Skills:** help uses Nature Lore, Honor or Endure Hardship; rob uses Sword & Shield or Cunning.
- **Renown:** help → Divinity or Renown; rob → Villainy with a treasure. Make robbing tempting.

### 3. Serve or oppose
- **Setup:** a lord, army or war-band wants your sword. For example: a baron raising men against his overlord, a sea-king recruiting for a raid, twin heirs each claiming their father's hall, a sellsword captain whose employer has refused to pay.
- **Fork:** join them / oppose them (sometimes join Arthur instead).
- **Skills:** Warfare, Mounted, Cunning (spying, sabotage), Diplomacy.
- **Renown:** siding against Camelot → Villainy; the fork often awards an **allegiance story token** (see story-tokens-and-renown.md).

### 4. The enchanted victim
- **Setup:** someone behaves strangely because of a spell. For example: a bishop who can speak only in birdsong, a knight who will not stop dancing, a bride who weeps pearls, a hound that speaks with its dead master's voice.
- **Fork:** break the spell / confront whoever cast it (or, darkly, take advantage).
- **Skills:** Magic, Piety, Wisdom to break it; a Martial skill to confront.
- **Renown:** Divinity for healing; Villainy for exploiting.

### 5. Clear the name or claim the bounty
- **Setup:** a fugitive swears they are innocent. For example: a charcoal-burner hunted for a murder in the woods, a lady's maid accused of stealing a ring, a young priest said to have raised the dead.
- **Fork:** help clear them / hand them in for the reward.
- **Skills:** Wisdom, Cunning (to find the truth), Hunting (to track the real culprit), Honor.
- **Renown:** clearing → Divinity or Renown; the bounty → Villainy with a treasure.

### 6. The hunt
- **Setup:** a hunter and a quarry. For example: a boar that has killed three huntsmen, a white hind the fay claim as their own, a wolf that walks on two legs at night, a salmon said to grant wisdom.
- **Fork:** join the hunt / save the quarry / challenge the hunter.
- **Skills:** Hunting, Nature Lore, Mounted, Endure Hardship.
- **Renown:** mixed. Sparing a sacred beast → Divinity.

### 7. The suitor's problem
- **Setup:** love in a knot. For example: a shy knight who wants you to deliver his love-letter (and has signed it with your name), sisters promised to the same man, a widow courted by her late husband's murderer.
- **Fork:** help the match / dissuade it / court someone yourself (romantic `*` option).
- **Skills:** Honor, Diplomacy, Cunning, Wisdom.
- **Renown:** Romance, with Villainy when the knight meddles cruelly or seduces the betrothed.

### 8. The wager, game or riddle
- **Setup:** play for something. For example: chess with a stranger for the use of his ferry, a drinking match in a mountain hall, a riddle contest with a talking raven, a wager on which of two knights will reach a chapel first.
- **Fork:** play by the rules / cheat / refuse and act directly.
- **Skills:** Cunning, Wisdom, Diplomacy.
- **Renown:** cheating → Villainy (and more treasure); honest play → Renown.

### 9. The holy test
- **Setup:** a miracle within reach. For example: a well that heals only the truthful, a saint's bones that will not be moved, a blind abbess who asks you to describe the Grail you have seen in dreams.
- **Fork:** attempt it / ask for the story / ask for a lesser blessing.
- **Skills:** Piety, often **or** a Divinity renown check.
- **Renown:** Divinity, sometimes with *lose all Villainy* on success.

### 10. The teller of tales
- **Setup:** someone with a story to tell: a bard, a ghost, an old soldier, a nun who was once a queen.
- **Fork:** hear the story (a no-roll result: lore plus a small reward) / learn their craft / court or challenge them.
- **Skills:** Wisdom, Honor, Diplomacy.
- **Note:** "ask about their history" is a signature option. Give the tale real lore: it rewards curiosity.

### 11. The fay bargain
- **Setup:** a gift or a request from the Otherworld. For example: a ferry of glass that crosses a river that isn't there, a fay child asking you to name her, a feast where every dish is your favourite and none of it is real.
- **Fork:** accept / question or refuse / give it to one side or the other / court the fay.
- **Skills:** Magic, Wisdom, Honor, Cunning.
- **Renown:** Divinity for resisting, Villainy for joining in mischief. Failures here earn Accursed, Ensorcelled, Beast Form or Mad.

### 12. The revenge request
- **Setup:** someone wronged wants blood. For example: a smith who wants the knight who killed his son challenged, an exiled queen raising a curse against her usurper, a monk who has kept a list of names for twenty years.
- **Fork:** help the revenge / stop it / redirect it (champion them, or reconcile).
- **Skills:** Sword & Shield, Diplomacy, Piety, Wisdom.
- **Renown:** helping murder → Villainy; championing a just cause → Romance or Renown.

### 13. The prank or the disguise
- **Setup:** mistaken identity, a disguise or a prank. For example: a knight fighting in borrowed arms, a steward who has been impersonating his dead lord, a travelling troupe that has billed you as the main event.
- **Fork:** expose them / play along / turn the joke back.
- **Skills:** Cunning, Mounted (a joust to settle it), Honor.
- **Renown:** light, often Renown or Romance.

### 14. The council of war
- **Setup:** an army before battle asks for your counsel, or a siege needs breaking.
- **Fork:** fight for this side / for the other / advise.
- **Skills:** Warfare, Cunning, Nature Lore (terrain), Mounted.
- **Renown:** by allegiance.

### 15. The monster with a reason
- **Setup:** a beast that isn't simply a beast. For example: a dragon that wants to be baptised, a sea-serpent guarding the drowned village it once belonged to, a wyvern mother defending a nest that the villagers want burned.
- **Fork:** attack / parley or take a subtler approach / (darkly) exploit it.
- **Skills:** a Martial skill against Wisdom, Diplomacy or Magic.
- **Renown:** mercy and understanding → Divinity; slaughter → Renown; exploitation → Villainy.

### 16. The charlatan
- **Setup:** a false sage, a pedlar of miracles, a braggart at a fair claiming your own deeds as his.
- **Fork:** expose them / play along / take a cut.
- **Skills:** Cunning, Wisdom, Sword & Shield (to challenge the braggart).
- **Renown:** exposing → Renown or Divinity; taking a cut → Villainy.

### 17. The lost one
- **Setup:** a child separated from a pilgrimage, a surveyor whose map has been enchanted, a novice who has run from her convent.
- **Fork:** guide or find them / bring them to justice / let them go.
- **Skills:** Nature Lore, Wisdom, Hunting, Piety.

### 18. The restless dead
- **Setup:** a drowned bride on a causeway, a hanged man asking for his name to be cleared, a spectral host that rides the same road every night.
- **Fork:** grant the request / refuse / lay the spirit to rest.
- **Skills:** Piety, Diplomacy, Magic.
- **Renown:** Divinity. Rudeness is punished: Pursued by spectral guards.

### 19. The captive
- **Setup:** someone held. For example: a bard in a baron's cage, a knight bound beneath a waterfall, a queen kept in a tower of her own design.
- **Fork:** rescue by force / by wit / bargain with the captor / leave them.
- **Skills:** any, by approach.

## Milieu patterns

Milieus have no named character card. The situation comes from the milieu and the terrain, and the people in it are invented. Milieu openings rely more on the **setting** than character openings do. Choices are usually **direct** (attack, enter, confront) against **indirect** (parley, find another way, take cover), sometimes with a **greedy** third choice that openly makes things worse.

| Milieu | What the book tends to make of it |
|---|---|
| **Perilous Cave** | a cave hiding treasure, knowledge or a saint's last message; a village menaced by what lives below; an island cave not on any map |
| **Mysterious Castle** | a castle not on your map; hospitality with a hidden condition; a siege underway; a figure watching from a window; golden light in a bog |
| **Strange Beast** | a city held hostage by a monster; a unicorn at a river; a giant nest on a ledge; a dragon in a watchtower; a fabled swamp horror; a sea serpent |
| **Raging Storm** | shelter shared with suspicious strangers; a storm that is someone's spell; rising water; a storm at sea; a storm used as cover for a crime |
| **Ruthless Brigands** | a chasm with a toll; a band camped by a village; an inn full of rogues; pirates and longships; outlaws holding a nobleman |
| **Clandestine Meeting** | a hooded knight's summons; druids in green and white; a gathering on a stormy heath; knights plotting; ships meeting in secret |
| **Abandoned Ruins** | a ruined fortress where the wind makes voices; a roofless temple; an ancient well ringed by trees; Roman ruins; a spire rising from the sea |
| **Harrowing Battlefield** | a pitched battle to join or stop; a siege; a tournament melee; Viking fleets; in Age 3, Arthur's and Mordred's hosts |
| **Magnificent Feast** | a festival; a fay revel through a stone arch; a mountain-top feast at midnight; a Samhain celebration; a witches' supper; an island tournament |

This table is ground the published book has already covered. Keep the milieu's premise, and find your own situation inside it.

See world-and-characters.md for how the three ages change the same milieu.

## Twists the book likes

Put one in about a third of encounters, usually at the resolution or in an outcome:

- The victim is the villain, or the villain is a victim.
- The monster was enchanted, provoked or grieving.
- The reward is cursed, or the curse is a blessing.
- The fool is wise, and the wise man is a fraud.
- The request is reasonable, and the reason for it is not.
- A comic scene turns suddenly deadly on a failed roll.
- Success saves one person and costs another (success with a status cost).

## Building a plot slate

Before writing a character's eleven encounters:

1. **Give each feature its dramatic mode** (see world-and-characters.md), then choose a pattern that fits it: Desperate suits a trial; Amorous suits the suitor's problem; Cunning suits a wager or a prank.
2. **Use at least six different patterns** across the eleven, so the block doesn't repeat itself.
3. **Balance the moral forks.** About half the encounters should offer a real Villainy road.
4. **Balance the skills.** Across a character's resolutions, try to touch all four categories.
5. **Thread two or three encounters** into your recurring cast or a story token, so the block connects to the rest of the book.
6. **Draw on legend for about a third**, entered at an unusual angle. Invent the rest.
